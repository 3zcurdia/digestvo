summary: >-
  Michael Lynch catalogs the anti-patterns he sees in developer blogs:
  meandering intros that delay the payoff, assuming readers share your
  background, links used instead of explanations, posts that open as sequels,
  stiff formal prose, and basic rendering failures like mobile page overflow
  and low-contrast text. His rule of thumb is that the title and the first
  three sentences must tell a reader who the post is for and what they will
  gain. He argues links should be a bonus rather than a prerequisite, urges
  writers to drop the formality and write the way they talk, and suggests
  checking each article in the browser's mobile preview before publishing.

Most commenters land on agreement, treating the list as a start and adding their
own anti-patterns: posts with no visible date (or no year), footnotes that force
scrolling, hotlinked images, and — in the top comment — spending two months
building a custom blogging platform and then never publishing anything. The
sharpest objection is the "overreliance on links" rule, because Simon Willison
loves linking and suspects nobody clicks links anymore; the thread converges on
mtlynch's refinement that an article should still make sense if no link is
clicked, with links offered as a bonus rather than a prerequisite. A second
flank targets "minimize assumptions about the reader's background": amw-zero
argues it should be "know your audience," since writing everything for a total
beginner is equally bad. The tips subthread also argues over preload/preconnect
hints, with chrismorgan calling them mostly unnecessary and gcupc defending
hyphenated, justified prose as the right default for long-form text.
