summary: >-
  New FCC rules that took effect October 13 make online marketplaces legally
  responsible for keeping unauthorized RF devices off their platforms, and
  eBay has responded by restricting listings of radio transmitting equipment.
  Sellers of equipment subject to FCC authorization must now submit an FCC ID
  to be posted with the listing, though exemptions cover existing listings,
  used gear and small sellers. After ARRL asked for clarification, eBay said
  ham-band-only radios are exempt because they need no FCC authorization,
  while two-way VHF/UHF gear that transmits outside amateur bands requires
  certification.

Most commenters land on the restriction being security theater, because the
affected gear has always shipped unlockable: Baofengs restore their full
transmit range with a key-combo factory reset, and $20 SDRs receive anywhere
anyway. The sharpest objection is superkuh's point that eBay's staff, not the
FCC, now decide what needs an FCC ID, so they will ban anything RF out of
liability risk, including $5 receive-only low-noise amplifiers and boards using
pre-certified LoRa modules. A sizeable minority defends the rule, arguing
unlocked Baofengs have long let unlicensed users step on police and fire
frequencies, and citing 47 U.S.C. 302a, which covers offering a device for sale,
so the FCC's authority over marketplaces is written into statute. The side
argument over the 800 MHz AMPS no-receive ban sharpens the deeper worry about
where equipment rules end and listening laws begin.
