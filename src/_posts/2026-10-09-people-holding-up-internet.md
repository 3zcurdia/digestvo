summary: >-
  The article downloads the full commit history of 23 projects that phones,
  browsers and servers depend on and counts who did the regular work, defined
  as ten or more changes between October 2025 and 2026, finding that 11 of the
  23 had only one or two people doing it. It profiles the individuals behind
  the time zone database (Paul Eggert, on four billion phones), xz (Lasse
  Collin), core-js, sudo, zlib, bash, HarfBuzz, curl, SQLite and
  libjpeg-turbo, recounting episodes like the xz backdoor and Heartbleed. It
  then maps that labour against money: Germany's Sovereign Tech Agency funds
  OpenSSL, curl and log4j, while the tz database, SQLite, zlib and xz draw
  nothing.

Most commenters land on the delivery rather than the data: the highest-scored
replies call the scroll-hijacking scrollytelling unreadable, one saying it
stopped page scroll on a phone and another saying it stopped rendering a quarter
of the way through, though a minority found the same format pleasant and
museum-like. The sharpest objection to the writing is the sentence about Denis
Pushkarev, where "two young women on a dark road ended up under his car" drew a
long sub-thread arguing the phrasing absolves him of a killing, countered by the
reply that the proposed active version simply says something different. On
substance there is little fight: rau calls the point solid and suggests ESR's
loadsharers list as an earlier attempt at the same problem, while zem wants a
corporate-sponsored foundation that would hire these maintainers as employees.
