  Meta's updated commercial terms, effective October 30, authorize it to use
  scrapers and test credentials to access websites associated with business
  accounts on Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. The language explicitly
  allows collected content to be used to train Meta products, including AI
  systems, and affects virtually every news publisher. Three legal experts
  told Nieman Lab the wording could support building a competing archive,
  bypassing registration walls, and overriding robots.txt, though training
  still faces unsettled fair-use questions. Meta says the update only
  consolidates existing terms and that robots.txt controls still apply,
  offering no opt-out short of leaving.
---

Most commenters treat the October 30 scraping clause as unenforceable overreach,
because no publisher reads a ToS update email and the only opt-out is abandoning
Facebook distribution entirely. The sharpest counter is that scraping public
sites needs no consent at all, so Meta is merely laundering an already-legal
practice into a copyright shield for AI training plus permission to mint test
credentials that bypass registration walls. A second fault line is antitrust
tying: if restaurants and newsrooms cannot afford to leave Meta's ad and
audience machine, consent is coercion, not agreement. The most cynical
prescription is to stay inside and slow-walk the machine, which others dismiss
as fantasy against Meta's scale.
