summary: >-
  A blog post argues that opening a reply with "Claude says" undermines the
  speaker: deferring to an LLM signals the topic is outside their expertise,
  and the reader has no way to judge whether the concern was even communicated
  properly. The author blames AI for runaway stack complexity, citing an
  example where a model proposed forking and maintaining every dependency from
  source instead of using an artifact repository's built-in patching feature.
  Prompting a chatbot, the post concludes, flatters a false sense of critical
  thinking, and feeding presuppositions into an LLM only poisons the well.
---

Most commenters land on agreeing that "Claude says" is tiresome while rejecting
the article's claim that AI caused our overengineered stacks, because creesch
argues the abstraction pile-up predates LLMs and grew from gatekeeping and
vendors selling acceleration. The live argument is what to do instead: pyfisch
reaches for Claude as a fast way to confirm a suspicion he is 80% sure of, and
pralkarz answers that the hour of manual research is the point, since vetting
sources and fact-checking are skills that atrophy when outsourced. The sharpest
objection is to gspr's "just send me the prompt you used", because tonyarkles
and rustybolt note that useful output arrives only after many rounds of steering
a session, so a first prompt conveys almost nothing — rustybolt's analogy being
that asking for the prompt is choosing the lottery ticket over the prize.
