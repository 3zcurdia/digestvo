  Rikab Gambhir's satirical guest post on Terence Tao's blog takes aim at
  OpenAI's release of 722 manuscripts from an internal frontier model and a
  physics effort that produced 2500 more write-ups. Writing as a theoretical
  physicist, the author mocks a research culture that treats "advancing
  humanity's knowledge" as stockpiling potentially true factoids nobody will
  read or understand, while outsourcing the tedious work of checking
  correctness to the mathematics community. Borges's Library of Babel is held
  up as the research institution the field is converging on: everything might
  be true, nothing needs to be legible. The piece closes by asking readers to
  remember why they chose science and math.

Most commenters land on the post's premise that the field is now sorting through
machine-generated slop, because dlisboa compares OpenAI's 722 manuscripts to the
spray-and-pray code dumps AI users already leave on open source projects for
communities to sift. The thread's real argument is ryan-duve's
craft-versus-productivity split, which he says LLMs have exposed by separating
two kinds of coders who always sat on the same teams; gspr extends it to
research, estimating a 99/1 craft-to-goal split among mathematicians and a 10/90
one among programmers, and that minority share terrifies him. The sharpest
objection is pm's, that "coding for the craft" is an euphemism for lack of
skill, since getting things done and writing good code are not actually in
tension. jcelerier answers that framing directly: for most working programmers
the practice is a monthly wage with less back pain than carrying cement bricks,
not a calling.
