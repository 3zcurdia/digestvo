  David Bushell argues that web professionals should ride out the current
  AI-driven slump by investing in skills that hype cycles cannot replace. His
  'in' list for a sustainable career covers accessibility as a discipline
  practised with real users, deep CSS knowledge instead of abstraction layers,
  plain communication skills, and openly rejecting far-right politics in tech.
  His 'out' list dismisses React as a legacy framework not worth learning
  today, GitHub as a liability in favour of self-hosted Forgejo, and developer
  influencers peddling a closed-web narrative. The premise is that people
  still need the web and professional demand will return.

Most commenters land on the post being wishful thinking, because the core bet —
that demand for professional web work comes back once this phase blows over —
rests on conviction rather than evidence, with the top replies calling it
admirable delusion. The sharpest objection is the call to drop React: hyperpape
points at the Stack Overflow survey showing React still dominant after 13 years,
while Two9A agrees learning it fresh is wasted time yet argues the real payoff
is maintaining the existing React inventory for PHP-and-Cobol-style maintenance
contracts. A second argument breaks over whether rejecting AI tools is coherent
on any ground other than moral ones, since coleifer calls their utility
unquestionable and the author replies his published AI policy already covers
that. On accessibility, dlisboa's claim that no employer in 20 years has treated
it seriously draws the reply that it only changes through regulation.
