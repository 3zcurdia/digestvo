  Mathematician and former Microsoft Research Lean team member Vi Hart
  examines OpenAI's claimed Lean-verified proof of the Navier-Stokes
  Millennium Prize problem. She explains that the breakthrough rests on years
  of human formalization in Mathlib and recent mathematical work by Tristan
  Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge, whom OpenAI allegedly scooped after observing
  their progress on its platform. Hart argues that generating formal proofs
  without human direction merely creates a meaningless Library of Babel rather
  than replacing mathematical insight. Finally, she outlines seven technical
  and social ways the verified proof could still fail scrutiny despite likely
  holding up.

Most commenters praise Hart's essay as measured, domain-informed analysis,
particularly her observation that LLM breakthroughs look far less magical once
you understand the years of human Mathlib formalization and mathematical
research underneath them. Rather than debating the Navier-Stokes proof itself,
the only friction in the six-comment discussion targets Patreon as a publishing
platform. Readers complain about a floating bottom widget that pops up on every
scroll and lingers for a full minute, distracting from the long-form essay.
Others reluctantly accept the clunky interface because few viable
creator-payment alternatives exist outside niche competitors like Steady, though
Firefox's Reader View cleanly strips the offending chrome.
