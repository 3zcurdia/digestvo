summary: >-
  DeepSeek has released DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, described in its model card
  as a minor update to DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash that delivers substantial
  improvements across reasoning, coding, agentic tasks, and visual benchmarks
  while reducing task execution time and output length. The 763B-parameter,
  MIT-licensed model scores 42.6 on HLE without tools, 42.3 on Terminal-Bench
  4.0, 77.0 on DeepSWE v1.1, and 60.5 on Automation-Bench, placing it above
  GLM-5.3 and GPT-5.6 Sol on several agentic coding benchmarks and near
  Anthropic's Opus 5, though still behind the frontier on HLE. It supports a
  1M-token context window and ships with a Python reference prompt encoder
  plus Rust-based deepseek-recipe libraries for converting OpenAI-style API
  requests into DeepSeek's conversation format.

Most commenters land on DeepSeek's cost-to-quality ratio being unmatched while
treating the release as strong but still short of the frontier, where Sol 6.1
and Opus 5.5 are considered smarter for hard tasks. One developer notes a $20
ChatGPT Plus plan exhausts its 5-hour limit in minutes, whereas $20 of DeepSeek
API spend goes orders of magnitude further. The sharpest objection is to the
benchmarks themselves: the top reply flatly doubts they "can't possibly be
real," and the thread never verifies them beyond the model card's own table,
where the model still trails Opus 5 on HLE (42.6 vs 56.3) and Terminal-Bench
(42.3 vs 51.8). A side debate turns on the card's contradictory "minor update"
with "substantial improvements" phrasing, with one commenter speculating Chinese
labs under-version deliberately to dodge CCP certification for major releases —
unverified, but it colors how the thread reads the rollout. Local running is
dismissed as impractical: at 763B parameters even quantized versions reportedly
need roughly four DGX Sparks, and quants degrade on long contexts. The mood is
genuine excitement about open weights closing on Opus-class performance,
tempered by benchmark skepticism.
