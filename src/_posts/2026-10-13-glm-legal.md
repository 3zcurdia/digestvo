  GLM-Legal is Z.ai's domain-specific legal model, built on the lightweight
  GLM-5.3-Flash foundation with mid-training on expanded legal corpora and
  reinforcement learning across tens of thousands of realistic legal
  environments co-developed with its agent harness. Z.ai reports a 19.2%
  all-pass rate on Harvey LAB across 24 practice areas, 12.5 points above the
  base model, plus parity with frontier closed models on LegalBench and
  PLawBench, evaluations Vals says it ran independently. The post shows
  side-by-side examples of contract review, evidence analysis and cross-border
  privacy planning, with edits landing in Word as tracked changes, and invites
  firms to apply for beta API access and try it in the AutoClaw desktop agent.

Most commenters land on the liability question, because the model's output still
requires a licensed lawyer who signs it. One camp argues that makes disclosure
of AI use mandatory: nobody should lose years of prison time because a lawyer
rubber-stamped a bad prompt, and clients should know the tool was used because
machine output is likelier to be buggy. The counter is that the lawyer is
already sanctioned, disbarred or sued for any mistake regardless of tooling, and
that courts should vet arguments better instead. The sharpest objection is that
GLM-Legal ships closed weights: several readers say a legal model firms cannot
self-host or audit is DOA for an industry that needs data residency, with one
estimating it would run on about $14k of hardware if released openly, while
others note the blog never addresses hallucinated cases, the failure mode that
has already drawn judicial sanctions.
