summary: >-
  Baldur Bjarnason argues that software developers and UX designers are doing
  badly: a wave of 'I'm leaving tech' videos, burnout, and a 30% drop in
  Icelandic computer-science enrolment. He blames executive incentives —
  smaller headcounts mean fewer stock options and less worker resistance —
  alongside work that has lost its novelty, since agent-built software is
  mostly old ideas in new containers. LLM coding feels like delegating to a
  clueless junior rather than automating, which strips away flow and
  investment in the output. He doubts a bubble collapse would fix it, because
  the tools only need to work for the precarity to persist, and suggests
  planning a life outside tech.

Most commenters land on the essay naming something real they feel themselves,
because the top-voted replies are confessions: the thread opens on 'being a
software developer is getting more and more depressing for every day that goes
by', and another poster adds a rhyming HUMANS.md to his repo as a symbolic act
against AGENTS.md. The sharpest objection is jaculabilis's, that the argument is
built on 'I keep seeing videos about this', which is exactly what an algorithm
fed on quitting content keeps serving you while engineers who enjoy their jobs
never film it. janus piles on the post hoc leap from AI adoption to Iceland's
enrolment drop, and thombles generalises it into 'you are what you read', with
lilac and tome insisting they are having more fun programming than ever. A
second argument runs underneath: jschuster says LLM use is environmentally and
ethically indefensible and employers offer only use it or leave, while
delirehberi answers that with no savings and a burnout to recover from, adapting
is the only option.
