summary: >-
  Rich Hickey argues that LLMs are not tools but automation. A tool, he
  writes, is something you wield: held in the hand, giving control, continuous
  direction and sensory feedback, and building expertise as you use it. LLMs
  offer none of that — you make a request and the model acts on its own, so
  using one to write code is abdicating writing code rather than directing it.
  He likens prompt-writing to gaming a drive-through order, and warns that
  what you skip by abdicating is learning to write, to argue, and to think.

Most commenters land on Hickey's underlying point but fight over his definition
of 'tool'. pmbauer argues the dictionary quibble misses the real claim: like
fast food, LLM use carries costs beyond the transaction that people ignore
because the short-term benefit is visible. The sharpest objection is cultpony's
— machines and computers already fail the hand-operated, single-purpose
definition Hickey leans on, so ruling LLMs out on those grounds proves nothing —
and tentacloids answers that reaching for whichever definition fits your side is
exactly why the debate stalls without common ground. A smaller strain doubts the
premise: one commenter says Hickey treats LLMs as only summarization and code
generation, while another singles out the line about loading your background
mind with fodder for novel ideas.
